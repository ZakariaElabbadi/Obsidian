# 🎯 Full Summary: Inbound Strategy Fundamentals

> Summary of 6 HubSpot Academy lessons: Inbound Fundamentals, Company Purpose, Setting Business Goals, Buyer Personas, Buyer's Journey, and the Flywheel Model.

---

## 1️⃣ What is #Inbound?

- **Inbound** is a business philosophy built around **helping people**, not forcing them to buy.
- It's about building meaningful 1:1 relationships with strangers, prospects, and customers.
- Core idea: **good-for-the-customer = good-for-the-business**
- Key stats:
  - 94% of consumers stopped communicating with a company due to irrelevant messaging.
  - 51% will never do business again with a company after one negative experience.
  - 93% are more likely to be repeat customers when service is remarkable.

### 🔄 The Inbound Methodology (Attract – Engage – Delight)
Applies to every team (marketing, sales, services):

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| **Attract** | Helpful, contextual content that builds trust and shows expertise |
| **Engage** | Build a relationship, solve problems with context, clarity, creativity |
| **Delight** | Deliver an outstanding experience that turns customers into promoters |

> **Note:** A customer isn't truly a customer until they've had the chance to leave and chose not to.

### 🧭 The 5 Inbound Principles
1. **Standardize for consistency** – give the same answer no matter who's asked
2. **Contextualize for relevance** – use prior interaction history to respond faster
3. **Optimize for clarity** – match the message to the strengths of each channel
4. **Personalize for impact** – tailor every conversation to the individual
5. **Empathize for perspective** – acknowledge emotions while providing guidance

### 🧰 Foundations for Doing Inbound
Inbound principles → Company purpose → Business goals → Buyer personas → Buyer's journey → **CRM** (a unified growth platform for every team).

## 2️⃣ Company Purpose (#CompanyPurpose)

- **Core question:** Why was your company founded? (e.g., Khan Academy, American Girl)
- Losing sight of your purpose can lead to the fate of the **US railroad industry** (they thought they were in the "railroad business" instead of the "transportation business").

### Simon Sinek's Golden Circle
- **What** → the most concrete layer
- **How** → what differentiates you
- **Why** → **the core of your purpose** – profit should be part of your "how," not your "why"

### Culture
- Culture = the decisions people make when no one else is watching.
- A written Culture Code helps attract the right people and keeps the company on track.

### Jobs to Be Done Theory (#JobsToBeDone)
- People "hire" products to get a job done (e.g., V8 hired as an alternative to eating vegetables, not just for thirst).
- **Job Story format:**
  > As a (who), when I am (situation), I want to (motivation/action), so I can (desired outcome)

- Job dimensions to evaluate: **Functional – Financial – Personal Identity – Social Appearance**

## 3️⃣ Setting Business Goals (#BusinessGoals)

- Without clear goals there's no strategy ("Hope is not a strategy" – James Cameron)

### OKRs (Objectives & Key Results)
- **Objective**: a qualitative, aspirational statement (where do you want to go?)
- **Key Results**: 1–3 numeric, time-bound, verifiable metrics (how will you get there?)

**Example:**
- Objective: Improve customer satisfaction
- KR1: Increase customer retention from 70% to 85%
- KR2: Achieve an NPS of 15

- **Ideal scoring range:** 60–70% (if you're consistently hitting 100%, aim higher)
- Two grading types: **Numeric (0 to 1.0)** or **Binary (0 or 1)**
- "No more than 5 priorities" rule (Verne Harnish)
- Goals cascade hierarchically: Leadership → Departments → Teams → Individuals

### The Three Horizons Framework
| Horizon | Share of effort | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Horizon 1** | ~70% | Sustaining and optimizing the current business |
| **Horizon 2** | ~20% | Transitional plays bridging present and future |
| **Horizon 3** | ~10% | Radical innovation, genuinely new business models |

### MSPOT (HubSpot's One-Page Annual Plan)
Mission → Serving → Plays (top 5 objectives) → Omissions (deprioritized items) → Targets (Key Results)

## 4️⃣ Buyer Personas (#BuyerPersonas)

- A **Buyer Persona** is a semi-fictional representation of an ideal customer, based on data, interviews, and educated guesses.
- HubSpot example: **Owner Ollie** (small business owner) vs. **Mary Marketer** (professional marketer) → HubSpot chose to focus solely on Mary.

### Who Should Build the Persona?
Marketing typically leads it, but input is needed from every team — sales, services, finance, legal — not marketing alone.

### Steps to Create a Persona
1. Define what information is needed (grounded in your company's purpose)
2. Identify the best sources:
   - **Historical data** (marketing, sales, customer data)
   - **Customer interviews** (~15 people per persona)
   - **Educated guesses** reached by consensus across teams
3. Review and update regularly (personas change as your market evolves)

> ⚠️ A persona is a launch pad, not a substitute for knowing the actual individual once a relationship is built.

## 5️⃣ The Buyer's Journey (#BuyerJourney)

- **Definition:** The active research process someone goes through leading up to a purchase.

### The Three Stages
| Stage | Buyer Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Experiencing a problem, trying to name/understand it |
| **Consideration** | Problem defined; researching all possible solution approaches |
| **Decision** | Solution strategy chosen; comparing vendors to make a final choice |

### How Each Team Uses It
- **Marketing:** creates content tailored to each stage (problem-focused for awareness, solution-focused for consideration, product-focused for decision)
- **Sales:** adapts the conversation based on the prospect's stage
- **Services:** self-serve knowledge content reassures decision-stage prospects; also applies the concept to the *customer* journey (no need to restart the whole journey for upsells/cross-sells)

## 6️⃣ The Flywheel Model (#Flywheel)

### Funnel vs. Flywheel
- **Funnel:** one-directional movement; momentum stops at the bottom.
- **Flywheel:** happy customers add energy that speeds up rotation (referrals, repeat purchases); unhappy customers slow it down.

### How to Accelerate the Flywheel
- Invest in **customer service** as much as in sales and marketing (Brian Halligan).
- Even departing customers should leave with a good impression — they may still refer others.

### Combining Funnels and Flywheels
- Individual processes (like the sales process) can still be visualized as funnels, but they're **part of a larger flywheel**.
- Look for big drop-offs between funnel stages (e.g., Closed-Won → Referral) to find cross-team improvement opportunities.

## 🔗 Overall Summary

```
Company Purpose (Why)
      ↓
Business Goals (OKRs)
      ↓
Buyer Personas
      ↓
Buyer's Journey
      ↓
Inbound Methodology (Attract-Engage-Delight)
      ↓
Flywheel = sustainable growth powered by happy customers
```

All these concepts connect: **without knowing why the company exists**, you can't set the right goals; **without clear personas**, you can't build an effective buyer's journey; and **without a defined buyer's journey**, you can't run the Inbound methodology that powers the Flywheel.
